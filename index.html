The research puzzle

A public symbol is visible.
Its meaning is not.

Imagine walking past a monument or along a street named after a former president.

Does that symbol represent national history, a personal memory, an official message — or something you barely notice?

The presence of a commemorative symbol does not tell us how citizens interpret it.

Comparative case selection · 1/2

Why compare these two countries?

Shared post-Soviet context

Both gained independence in 1991 and were led for decades by their first presidents under political systems marked by concentrated presidential power.

Kazakhstan

Nursultan Nazarbayev

President from independence until 2019.

Uzbekistan

Islam Karimov

Comparing the cases helps me explore how citizens make sense of presidential commemoration in two related, but distinct, national contexts.
Research question
How do citizens in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan make sense of the public commemoration of their first presidents, Nursultan Nazarbayev and Islam Karimov?
Monuments Place names Memorial sites Official ceremonies
Epistemological positioning

Why interpretivism?

Meaning rather than measurement

I want to understand how people interpret public commemoration, rather than measure support for former presidents.

Participants’ own perspectives

Open-ended interviews allow participants to describe what commemorative symbols mean to them, without assuming one shared interpretation.

The same monument may carry different meanings for different people — including little or no personal significance.

Research gap · 1/2

What do we already know?

Official commemoration

Research examines how states construct commemorative practices, monuments and public memory.

Citizens’ memories and interpretations

Other studies explore how people remember historical events or engage with public monuments.

These strands do not fully explain how citizens interpret the specific public commemoration of their countries’ former presidents.
Research gap · 2/2

What will my study add?

An interpretivist comparison of how citizens in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan make sense of commemorative symbols associated with Nazarbayev and Karimov.

Expectations

What I expect — and could be wrong about

01

Participants may attach different meanings to the same commemorative symbol.

02

Personal experiences and political context may shape how participants explain these meanings.

03

Some participants may attach little significance to commemorative practices.

These expectations are open to revision through participants’ accounts.

Research transparency · 1/2

My position as a researcher

My perspective

As a Kazakhstani political science student, I may be more familiar with Kazakhstan’s commemorative practices than Uzbekistan’s.

My responsibility

I will use open-ended questions and reflexive notes to consider how my background and participants’ assumptions may shape the interviews.

Research transparency · 2/2

Working with AI: help and limitations

How AI helped

AI helped me refine my question, organize the literature and structure the presentation. I checked the sources and made the final research decisions.

What I learned — and what remains unclear

Working with AI helped me distinguish interpreting meanings from measuring attitudes. I am still considering how to make the two-country interview design feasible and comparable.
